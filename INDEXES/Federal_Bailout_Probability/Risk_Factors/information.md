Key risk factors that increase the probability of a future federal bailout for public pensions include:
- Chronic underperformance: Over the past 24 years, public pension funds have earned an average annual return of 6.62%, consistently below their assumed rates (which averaged 6.87% in 2024). This shortfall has been a primary driver of unfunded liabilities.
- Aggressive assumptions: Many plans continue to set optimistic assumed rates of return. A 1% miss can generate significant new debt, as highlighted by experts (e.g., Josh McGee: "if they miss their 7% return by just a little bit it will not be cost neutral—it will have a significant, meaningful cost to the city").
- Contribution inadequacy: In 2024, 54% of all employer contributions went to paying down legacy debt rather than funding new benefits. Contribution gaps allow debt to compound over time.
- Risky asset allocation: Pension portfolios have shifted heavily toward alternative assets (private equity, hedge funds, real estate) seeking higher returns, which increases volatility and opacity.
- Dangerous feedback loop: Unrealistic return assumptions → underfunding → pressure to take on more investment risk → greater vulnerability to market shocks → higher unfunded liabilities → even more unrealistic assumptions.
- Demographic pressures: An aging population increases benefit payouts while the active-to-retiree ratio declines, straining the funding model.
These factors together create a scenario where a severe economic downturn could overwhelm state and local budgets, making a federal rescue politically and economically compelling.
