

### Executive Summary
The U.S. financial system operates under a structural tension: Dodd-Frank statutory policy aims to eliminate bailout expectations, yet historical precedent (2008) and ongoing emergency interventions demonstrate that systemic crises will likely trigger massive government responses regardless of stated intent. The core challenge is that the post-2008 framework has improved resilience but not eliminated the need for backstops, while elevated debt (121.6% of GDP) simultaneously reduces fiscal capacity to absorb future crises while increasing political pressure on central bank independence.

### Key Findings

- **Bailout Paradox Persists**: FSOC's statutory mandate is to promote market discipline by eliminating expectations that the government will shield shareholders/creditors from losses, yet each recent stress event (2008, COVID, 2023 banking stress) has required government intervention. The 2008 interventions alone had "potential to increase future borrowings by more than $1 trillion" (2008 Citizen's Guide).

- **Debt Creates Fiscal-Monetary Blur**: With net interest outlays approaching $1 trillion and absorbing ~20% of federal revenues, high debt levels make monetary policy increasingly consequential for fiscal space, raising concerns that "fiscal considerations may constrain central bank actions even in the absence of formal institutional changes" (BIS Working Paper No. 1328).

- **Bailout Size Is Critical**: Japan's 1990s crisis provides empirical evidence that "too-small recapitalizations may even be damaging for bank lending policies"—undercapitalized banks evergreen bad loans and reduce credit to viable borrowers. Adequate bailouts increase credit supply; inadequate ones create "zombie" firms and prolong stagnation (FDIC Working Paper).

- **Regulatory Erosion Undermines Safety Net**: The CFA Institute Systemic Risk Council warns that weakening GSIB capital requirements "would dismantle key post-crisis safeguards" just as Basel III "endgame" implementation faces delays, creating a window of vulnerability.

- **Post-Crisis Reforms Have Limits**: While capital requirements, stress testing, and resolution planning have generally held, "each stress event has required some form of government intervention or backstop," revealing both the value and limits of existing safeguards (CFA Institute, Summer 2025).

### Contradictions / Debates

- **Bailout Intent vs. Reality**: FSOC's stated purpose is eliminating government backstop expectations, but Treasury's own reports document repeated interventions. The 2008 Citizen's Guide acknowledges "the largest financial effects...have not yet appeared...but they will show up" in future budgets.
- **Regulatory Retreat**: Weakening GSIB capital standards is justified by some as encouraging Treasury market liquidity, but CFA Institute analysis finds "data show that U.S. Treasury holdings have increased despite current leverage rules," undercutting that rationale.
- **Japan Lesson on Bailout Adequacy**: Policy debates often focus on whether to bail out, but the empirical evidence from Japan suggests the size question matters more—small bailouts can be worse than no bailout at all.

### Gaps / Uncertainties

- **AI and Technology Risk**: FSOC's new AI working group reflects recognition that "the speed of financial information, trading technology, and interconnectivity of financial firms acts as an accelerant for systemic vulnerability" but frameworks remain nascent.
- **NBFI Systemic Exposure**: The "immense growth in non-bank financial intermediaries represents a structural vulnerability not adequately addressed by traditional bank-centric regulation" yet solutions are underdeveloped.
- **Fiscal Limit Timing**: BIS models suggest debt-to-GDP ratio can "push toward its fiscal limit" under rate increases, but the exact threshold remains uncertain and market confidence is difficult to model.
- **Household Resilience Metrics**: FSOC's new household resilience working group signals awareness of financial stress beyond banking, but early-warning indicators and intervention thresholds remain undefined.
